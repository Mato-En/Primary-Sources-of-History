The Epic of Gilgamesh is a story about the king of Uruk, who befriends Enkidu and together they face several tribulations. After Enkidu dies, he is frightened of death and seeks eternal life. This quest is not successful and he returns home empty handed.

This epic provides a glimpse into cultural values and practices at that time, like the relationship to the gods or the roles of women. The following citations are taken from the translation by Maureen Gallery Kovacs available on the [Uruk Warka website](https://uruk-warka.dk/Gilgamish/The%20Epic%20of%20Gilgamesh.pdf)

As in most ancient narratives, the gods play a central role in the story. There is an ambivalence in the portrayal of the Mesopotamian gods. On the one hand, some gods show few regards to the life of humans, as the god Ea speaks to Enlil after a flood has wiped out most of humanity: "how could you bring about a Flood without consideration." (p.47) or Ishtar threatening to open the gates of the Netherworld such that the dead go up to eat the living (see p.23). On the other hand, they are also capable of showing compassion and goodwill toward the people (eternal life, saving Utna, grass and grain)
